=== Base Rate Neglect (id: base-rate-neglect) ===
Definition: When forming probability judgments, people underweight or ignore the prior probability (base rate) of an event in favor of individuating case-specific information that is vivid or descriptively rich. Bayesian reasoning requires integrating both; neglect of the prior systematically biases posterior estimates.
Mechanism: Vivid, individuating description is more cognitively available and feels more diagnostic than abstract population statistics, so the prior probability gets crowded out of the judgment entirely.

Identification criteria:
  - A probability or category judgment relies entirely on vivid individuating detail with no mention of the relevant population base rate
  - Base rate information that is available and relevant is omitted even though it would materially change the conclusion
  - Confidence in the case-specific conclusion is not adjusted downward despite a base rate that would argue against it

Linguistic signals:
  - Profile or trait lists used to settle a categorical judgment ('she's meticulous, soft-spoken... she must be a librarian')
  - Confident category assignment with no statistical caveat
  - Absence of phrases like 'most people in this situation are actually...' or any population-frequency framing
