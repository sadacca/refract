=== Anchoring (id: anchoring) ===
Definition: The first numerical value or piece of information encountered (the 'anchor') has a disproportionate influence on subsequent estimates, judgments, and interpretations. Adjustment from the anchor is typically insufficient.
Mechanism: Initial information activates a reference point; subsequent processing adjusts from that point but rarely adjusts far enough. The anchor primes semantically related information, making anchor-consistent evidence more accessible. Works even with clearly arbitrary anchors (Ariely et al., 2003).

Identification criteria:
  - The first number mentioned in an article establishes the frame through which all subsequent numbers are interpreted
  - A high (or low) initial figure is presented and subsequent discussion is structured as departures from that number rather than from a neutral baseline
  - Price, cost, or quantity negotiations are described starting from one party's opening position, which then frames the outcome as a concession or a win relative to that anchor
  - A statistic is cited that is technically accurate but selected because it makes a figure seem large (or small) relative to a less prominent alternative representation
  - Historical comparison is anchored to a specific prior period that makes the current figure appear unusually high or low

Linguistic signals:
  - Opening numbers followed by relative framing: '$10 billion ask' → 'settled for $6 billion' (anchor was $10B)
  - Comparisons structured as departures: 'down from', 'up from', 'compared to last year's record'
  - Initial framing of magnitude: 'as many as X' or 'at least X' before a more precise figure
  - Selective historical reference: 'the highest since [year]' where the comparison year was chosen to maximize apparent magnitude
