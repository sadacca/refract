=== Availability Heuristic (id: availability-heuristic) ===
Definition: The tendency to estimate the frequency or probability of an event based on how easily examples come to mind, rather than on base rates or statistical evidence. Cognitively, retrieval fluency is mistaken for prevalence: what is vivid, recent, or emotionally charged is retrieved faster and therefore judged more common.
Mechanism: Ease of retrieval from memory is used as a proxy for probability. Vivid, recent, or emotionally charged examples are retrieved faster and that retrieval speed is misread as evidence of how common the event actually is.

Identification criteria:
  - Frequency or risk claims are supported by one or a few salient anecdotes rather than base-rate or population-level data
  - An anecdote is used to imply a trend without any trend data accompanying it
  - Personal or recent exposure to examples ('everyone I know', 'three stories this week') stands in for systematic evidence
  - Statistical or epidemiological context that would contradict the anecdote-driven impression is absent or dismissed

Linguistic signals:
  - 'It's clearly become an epidemic' / 'happening all the time' without citation
  - Anecdote stacking: multiple personal stories offered as proof of prevalence
  - Dismissal of statistics in favor of personal impression: 'I don't care what the data says'
  - Recency anchoring: 'this summer', 'lately', 'recently' used as evidence of an ongoing pattern
