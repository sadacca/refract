=== Framing Effect (id: framing-effect) ===
Definition: Identical information presented in different ways produces systematically different responses. People react differently to information depending on whether it is framed as a gain or a loss, as a percentage or an absolute number, in positive or negative terms.
Mechanism: Loss aversion amplifies negative frames; the emotional valence of the frame activates System 1 responses before System 2 analysis can apply. Reference point selection determines what counts as 'gain' vs. 'loss'.

Identification criteria:
  - A statistic is presented in a form that emphasizes its magnitude (or minimizes it) compared to a neutral presentation: e.g., '90% survival rate' vs. '10% mortality rate'
  - An event is described using gain language ('recovered', 'saved', 'improved') or loss language ('failed', 'lost', 'worsened') when the neutral facts support neither exclusively
  - A reference point is implicitly chosen to make the current situation look better or worse than a different reference point would
  - The same policy, person, or event is described in terms that carry emotional valence (e.g., 'tax relief' vs. 'tax cut'; 'illegal alien' vs. 'undocumented migrant') that predisposes the reader to a particular interpretation
  - Comparisons are made to a selectively chosen baseline that makes the subject look better or worse than alternative baselines would

Linguistic signals:
  - Gain/loss framing: 'saved' vs. 'lost', 'recovered' vs. 'failed', 'rose' vs. 'fell'
  - Percentage vs. absolute framing: '5%' vs. '1 in 20' vs. '5 million people'
  - Loaded terminology without attribution: 'regime', 'crisis', 'surge', 'crisis', 'explosion'
  - Baseline selection: 'compared to last year' vs. 'compared to the pre-pandemic peak'
  - Relative vs. absolute risk framing: 'doubles your risk' (relative) for a 0.001% → 0.002% change (absolute)
