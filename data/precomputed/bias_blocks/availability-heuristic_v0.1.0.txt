=== Availability Heuristic (id: availability-heuristic) ===
Definition: Judging the likelihood or frequency of an event by how easily examples come to mind. Vivid, recent, emotionally salient, or heavily reported events are overestimated in frequency; boring, distant, or underreported events are underestimated.
Mechanism: Ease of retrieval from memory is used as a proxy for probability. The brain conflates 'I can think of many examples' with 'this happens often.' Media coverage amplifies availability: repeated exposure to stories about rare events makes those events feel common.

Identification criteria:
  - A rare event is described as representative or prevalent based on recent high-profile cases rather than statistical base rates
  - The article's implicit probability estimate for an event is driven by the number of examples cited rather than by population-level statistics
  - Anecdotes about vivid individual cases are used to support claims about frequency or typicality without base rate data
  - The article covers an event that has recently been heavily in the news and presents it as an escalating trend without trend data
  - Risk is described in absolute terms (number of cases) without reference to the denominator (total population, total incidents of similar events)

Linguistic signals:
  - Trend claims without trend data: 'increasingly', 'more and more', 'growing number of' without citation
  - Anecdote-as-data: using one or two vivid stories to imply broader prevalence
  - Denominator neglect: raw counts without rates (e.g., '200 attacks' without 'per 100,000 people')
  - Recency anchoring: 'since [recent high-profile event]' as evidence of a pattern
  - Typicality language: 'this is what happens when...', 'stories like this are becoming common'
