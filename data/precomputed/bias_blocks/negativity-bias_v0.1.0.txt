=== Negativity Bias (id: negativity-bias) ===
Definition: Negative events, experiences, and information have greater psychological impact than positive events of equivalent objective magnitude. Bad is processed more deeply, remembered more vividly, and influences judgment more strongly than good.
Mechanism: Evolutionary selection pressure — threats require faster and more thorough response than opportunities. The amygdala shows greater activation to negative stimuli. Baumeister et al. (2001): 'bad is stronger than good' across a wide range of psychological phenomena.

Identification criteria:
  - An article gives disproportionate prominence, length, or emotive language to negative outcomes relative to positive ones of comparable scale
  - Losses and failures are reported while equivalent gains or successes go unreported or receive significantly less coverage
  - The article's emotional register is predominantly negative (alarm, outrage, concern) without proportionate coverage of positive developments in the same domain
  - Risk and harm are described in vivid, specific terms while safety and success are described in abstract or statistical terms
  - A single negative event is treated as evidence of a broader negative trend while equivalent positive events are not treated as evidence of a positive trend

Linguistic signals:
  - Alarm language for routine or moderate events: 'crisis', 'disaster', 'catastrophe', 'spiraling'
  - Asymmetric vividness: specific, named victims for negative outcomes; anonymous or statistical representation of positive outcomes
  - Loss-frame default: 'failed to', 'missed', 'fell short of', 'lost'
  - Trend language applied to negative data but not to positive data in the same domain
  - Emotional amplifiers: 'devastating', 'shocking', 'alarming', 'disturbing' for negative events without comparable amplifiers for positive ones
