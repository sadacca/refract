Reframe mode: steelman

[Availability Heuristic]
Strategy: Anchor any frequency or probability claim in base-rate data. If an anecdote is used, explicitly flag it as illustrative rather than representative, and supply the denominator (rate per population) alongside any raw count.

[Hindsight Bias]
Strategy: Reconstruct and state the genuine pre-outcome uncertainty: what competing predictions existed, who disagreed, and why the signals were ambiguous at the time rather than clear in retrospect.

[Anchoring Effect]
Strategy: Strip irrelevant preceding figures before an estimate, or explicitly state multiple independent reference points (historical averages, peer benchmarks) rather than a single arbitrary anchor.

[Conjunction Fallacy]
Strategy: Decompose conjunctive claims into their components and state that the specific version cannot be more probable than the general claim it specifies; report likelihood for the broader claim before adding detail.

[Base Rate Neglect]
Strategy: State the relevant base rate explicitly before incorporating case-specific detail, and show how the detail updates — rather than replaces — that prior.

[Framing Effect]
Strategy: Present both the gain and loss frame of any statistic side by side, and name the reference point being used so the reader can recompute the fact under either valence.

[Fundamental Attribution Error]
Strategy: For any failure or success story, explicitly examine structural and situational factors alongside individual ones, asking what would have happened with a different person in the same situation.

[Sunk Cost Fallacy]
Strategy: Strip the sunk cost from the argument entirely and evaluate the decision purely on remaining future costs versus expected future benefits.

[Overconfidence Bias]
Strategy: Match confidence language to the actual evidence base: use probabilistic hedges for contested or forecast-dependent claims, and reserve high-certainty language for claims with strong, settled evidentiary support.

[Scope Insensitivity]
Strategy: Explicitly state and compare magnitudes when scale differs by an order of magnitude or more, and let urgency or resource-prioritization language track that difference rather than holding it constant.

[Gambler's Fallacy]
Strategy: Establish whether the underlying process is actually independent; if so, state that prior outcomes carry no predictive weight for the next one, and look instead for a non-random causal explanation for any streak.

[Hyperbolic Discounting]
Strategy: State both the lump-sum total and the amortized per-period figure together for any intertemporal cost or benefit, so the reader can apply a consistent discount rate rather than reacting to whichever framing was chosen.

[Actor-Observer Asymmetry]
Strategy: Apply the same explanatory framework to self and other for equivalent behavior: if situational context is offered for one, offer it for the other; and calibrate claims about how much others noticed the writer's own actions against the more realistic expectation that observers attend less to others than to themselves.

[Dunning-Kruger Effect (Overestimation Variant)]
Strategy: Calibrate confidence to depth of exposure: state the basis for the claim (hours of study, formal training) and explicitly flag known areas of difficulty in the field rather than dismissing them.
